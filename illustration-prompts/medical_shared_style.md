# Shared style — medical illustration

Prepend this to every **medical** illustration prompt in this directory (not `accident.md`).

## What this is

Story illustration for *Pulses and Pathways*: each plate shows a beat of the OR narrative (exposure, clamp, suture, Doppler, close). Graphite/charcoal pencil on textured off-white paper.

**Evoke** a medical textbook illustration — paper grain, careful hatching, calm clinical focus — without being a teaching atlas plate for the OR story beats. Those plates carry **no** on-image labels or callouts; the play carries the explanation. The stenosis cutaway is the exception: annotated anatomy with crisp typeset labels (see `07_stenosis_cutaway.md`).

Stay **true to the procedure described in that beat**: forearm ulnar artery repair under regional block—exposure with retractors and suction, proximal vascular clamping, microvascular anastomosis, sterile Doppler check of the repair, then skin closure with adhesive. Do not swap in a different operation, wrong tool for the action, or a step that hasn’t happened yet.

Anatomy and instruments must be **accurate enough not to jar**: real forearm layers, vessels as tubes, recognizable clamps/retractors/suture tools/Doppler probe. Prefer plausible restraint over textbook completeness.

## Style

- Hand-drawn graphite and charcoal on cream paper grain
- Cross-hatching, stippling, visible pencil strokes
- Grayscale unless a plate allows selective deep red for a vessel
- Focus on the story beat; peripheral drapes/OR can stay looser

## Must not contain

- Labels, captions, handwriting, figure numbers, leader lines, arrows — **except** on the stenosis cutaway plate, which uses crisp typeset labels only
- Artist signature, watermark, logo, date stamp
- Photoreal CGI, stock-photo OR photography, UI frames
- Shock gore; keep blood restrained
- Readable monitor text or brand names on devices
- Anatomical nonsense (wrong tool for the action, cartoon vessels, impossible layering)
- Wrong procedure or wrong step (e.g. cautery instead of Doppler; open chest/abdomen instead of forearm; closing skin before the vessel is repaired)
- Comic, handwritten, or rounded “fun” label lettering on any annotated plate
